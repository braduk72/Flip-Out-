# Story Ideas

## Working Project Direction

This is the current story/world idea for the evolving Flip Out / Morris & Maurice project.

The project has moved away from being just a simple matching-pairs card game. The stronger direction is now a family-friendly puzzle adventure where a real cat dreams himself into strange, handmade worlds, and the puzzle mechanics drive the journey.

Core idea:

- Real-world Morris is asleep on his owner’s sofa.
- The TV is playing in the background.
- TV documentary details become distorted into Morris’s dream.
- The dream world begins in black-and-white / 2D and then becomes full-colour / 3D claymation-style after Morris is pulled into the adventure.
- The goal is simple: Morris wants to get back to his sofa.

---

## Real World Setup

Morris is a real-world tuxedo cat based on Gizmo.

He is curled up asleep on his owner’s sofa while the television plays in the background.

The TV documentary talks about:

- Tatianna Sloane
- A missing archaeologist / explorer
- The lost Inca “City of Gloom”
- The mythical “Pillar of Truth”

Morris is not actively listening. He is asleep, but his dream absorbs and distorts what he hears.

---

## Dream Transformation

In Morris’s dream, the TV documentary becomes:

- Tatianna Sloane → Catty Anna Sloane
- City of Gloom → Temple of Gloom
- Pillar of Truth → Collar of Tooth

The dream world is not realistic. It follows cat-and-mouse dream logic.

The worlds are built from things Morris might know from the house, from food, toys, packaging, TV, stories, and cat/mouse instincts.

## Morris / Maurice

### Real World
Name: Morris  
Based on: Gizmo  
Appearance: black-and-white tuxedo cat

### Dream World
Name: Maurice  
Personality: posh British cat with attitude  
Goal: get back to his sofa  
Role: straight man / reluctant hero

Maurice is no longer a detective and no longer “M.E.” in the main story direction.

He is just a slightly pompous, comfortable, well-fed cat who wants to get home.

He has a posh voice and stiff-upper-lip attitude until trouble becomes unavoidable.

He does not eat mice.

He is horrified by the very thought of eating mice. If he were less posh, the idea would make him retch.

His preferred food is:

“Poulet avec gravy”

Which is just chicken, but he refuses to call it that because he is not a savage.

---

## Sprockett

Sprockett is a fast-talking New York mouse.

He is comic relief, not the main villain.

He talks quickly, has a streetwise attitude, and is very funny against Maurice’s posh British seriousness.

He may call Maurice “Grubbs,” which Maurice dislikes.

Sprockett becomes Maurice’s unwanted companion.

The two should work as a comedy duo:

- Maurice: posh, serious, irritated, dignified
- Sprockett: loud, chaotic, cheeky, practical, impossible to get rid of

---

## First Meeting / Accidental Rescue

Maurice is crossing a bridge over the Cheddar Gorge.

The bridge is made from breadsticks, crackers, and other mouse-world food materials.

Halfway across, a scream is heard in the distance:

“AAAAAAARRRGGHHHHHHH!”

It gets louder and louder.

Sprockett falls from above and lands directly on Maurice.

Maurice accidentally breaks Sprockett’s fall.

Sprockett insists Maurice saved his life.

Maurice strongly disagrees.

Key running gag:

Sprockett:
“You saved me!”

Maurice:
“I did no such thing.”

Sprockett:
“You broke my fall!”

Maurice:
“You assaulted me from orbit.”

This line is canon and should be kept.

Possible later reveal: Sprockett may actually have fallen from the Moon of Stilton, making “from orbit” technically true.

## Temple of Gloom

The Temple of Gloom is built by mice.

It should not be a normal stone temple.

It is a mouse temple, so it is made from cheese and food-based materials.

Features:

- Walls made from giant blocks of cheese
- Pillars made from stacked cheese wheels
- Bridges made from crackers / breadsticks
- Slabs made from little square Cheeselet-style biscuits
- Rivers of molten cheese
- The Cheddar Gorge
- Cheese idols, cheese carvings, mouse statues
- Mouse-built architecture treated with absolute seriousness by mice

Maurice finds all of this absurd.

The mice do not.

---

## The Cheddar Gorge

The pit/chasm is not a generic pit.

It is the Cheddar Gorge.

This is both a cheese joke and a reference-style name.

The Cheddar Gorge may contain:

- Rivers of molten cheddar
- Fondue flows
- Biscuit stepping stones
- Cracker bridges
- Cheese waterfalls
- Cheese bubbles and steam
- Dangerous sticky melted cheese hazards

## Monterey Jack

Monterey Jack is no longer just a joke phrase.

He is now a legendary mouse explorer.

He is the mouse-world equivalent of a great adventurer.

He disappeared while searching for:

The Fondue of Cheeses

This gives the mouse civilisation mythology and purpose.

“You don’t know Monterey Jack!” becomes the mouse version of “You don’t know Jack!”

---

## The Fondue of Cheeses

The Fondue of Cheeses is the legendary treasure / sacred source sought by Monterey Jack.

It may be:

- A mythic cheese source
- The origin of the Sacred Cheese Flow
- The treasure hidden deep within the Temple of Gloom
- The mouse equivalent of the Holy Grail

The mice take it very seriously.

Maurice thinks it is melted cheese.

## Future Level: The Moon of Stilton

The next obvious dream world is the Moon.

Everyone knows the moon is made of cheese.

The mice know the truth:

It is actually a giant wheel of Stilton.

It stinks terribly.

The reason characters need suits is not because of space or lack of oxygen.

They need suits because of:

The Odour of Everlasting Socks

Possible features:

- Stilton craters
- Blue cheese caves
- Cheese dust storms
- Cracker meteors
- Old Monterey Jack expedition clues
- Odour Protection Suits
- Mouse moon civilisation
- Cheese-based moon gravity jokes

## Art Direction

The new likely direction is claymation / stop-motion style.

Important visual idea:

- Morris / Maurice is clay
- Clothes are real fabric
- Worlds look handmade
- Materials should feel tactile and physical
- Imperfections are welcome
- The set can feel like a handmade TV studio / tabletop world

However, if using Unreal Engine, the smarter workflow is:

- Build characters and locations as reusable assets
- Use AI for concept art, textures, references, and mood boards
- Do not rely on image generation to keep characters or rooms consistent across shots

## Game Structure

The original Flip Out concept was matching pairs.

The stronger direction is now broader:

Flip Out means flipping things:

- cards
- slabs
- biscuit tiles
- switches
- lights
- blocks
- bridges
- dream-world mechanisms

Puzzle types can include:

- Matching pairs
- Lights Out style puzzles
- Shape puzzles
- Colour puzzles
- Tile memory
- Path building
- Boss rooms
- Turn-based cat vs mouse mechanics

---

## Bridge / Slab Mechanics

The gameplay can still use matching-pair or puzzle logic, but now it has story purpose.

Maurice is trying to cross a hazard.

The player flips or solves blocks to create a safe route.

In the Temple of Gloom:

- The blocks are food/cheese/biscuit based
- The hazards are molten cheese / Cheddar Gorge
- Mice can sabotage the route
- Sprockett may initially be antagonist but later becomes companion

Earlier idea:

- Cat had 9 lives
- Mouse had Cheese Wheel of Life with 8 wedges

This may still be useful for game modes, boss levels, or multiplayer, even if the story direction now makes Sprockett a companion.

## Current Best Summary

A comfortable tuxedo cat named Morris falls asleep on his sofa while a TV documentary plays. His dream transforms the documentary into a bizarre claymation cat-and-mouse adventure where he becomes Maurice, a posh British cat trapped in mouse-built worlds of cheese, crackers, molten cheddar and ridiculous mythology. He accidentally saves Sprockett, a fast-talking New York mouse, and the two are forced to travel together through dream worlds as Maurice tries desperately to get back to his sofa.

Core emotional engine:

Maurice wants peace, dignity, and his sofa.

Sprockett wants adventure, cheese, and someone to annoy.

Everything else is the dream trying to stop them.

## Cat Religion vs Mouse Religion

An important worldbuilding idea:

The mice follow a cheese-based religion.

Examples:

- Cheeses
- In Cheeses' Name!
- Sweet Baby Cheeses!
- Sacred Cheese Flow
- Fondue of Cheeses
- Temple of Gloom
- Monterey Jack

Maurice does NOT share these beliefs.

Maurice is a cat.

Therefore Maurice's religious references are fish-based.

Examples:

- Cod Almighty
- Cod help me
- Cod preserve us
- Cod willing
- By the Holy Haddock
- For the love of Mackerel

This is not a typo.

It is intentional worldbuilding.

### Example Dialogue

Mouse Priest:
"In Cheeses' Name!"

Crowd:
"EDAM!"

Maurice:
"Cod Almighty..."

Mouse Priest:
"Who?"

Maurice:
"Cod."

Mouse Priest:
"Never heard of him."

Maurice:
"The Great Fish in the Sky."

Mouse Priest:
"You're making that up."

Maurice:
"Says the fellow worshipping a fondue."

## Correction: Fishing Boat Already on the Moon

Important correction:

Maurice and Sprockett emerge from the steam funnel of the fishing boat.

The reveal is that the boat is **already on the Moon of Stilton**.

They do not travel to the moon by boat.

They come out of the funnel and slowly realise the entire fishing boat is operating on the surface of the moon.

This strengthens the dream logic:

- The boat is fishing for cod
- The sea/location is tied to the North Sea of Tranquillity
- The moon is a giant wheel of Stilton
- Maurice encounters what may be Almighty Cod there
- The situation makes no normal sense, because it is Morris's dream

Possible reveal beat:

Maurice:
"Why is there a fishing boat on the moon?"

Sprockett:
"Where else ya gonna fish for moon cod?"

Maurice:
"...Cod help me."

## Sea of Tranquillity / Sea of Stars

The Moon of Stilton can include lunar seas.

### Sea of Tranquillity
Works as a moon pun and location.

### Sea of Stars
Another possible lunar sea / dream-ocean area.

The Sea of Stars is filled with literal **star fish**.

This can be both beautiful and silly:
- glowing starfish drifting in moonlit water
- star-shaped fish swimming among reflections of space
- constellations mirrored in the sea
- fish that look like tiny living stars
- possible navigation puzzle using star fish patterns

This connects the moon/space theme with cat-and-fish dream logic.

## Night Mares Falling Puzzle

After fleeing the Night Mares on the Moon of Stilton, Maurice and Sprockett jump off the moon.

This leads into a falling puzzle.

### Setup
Maurice and Sprockett are falling from the top of the screen through space / dream sky.

Below them is danger. If they fall off the bottom of the screen, they lose a life.

### Core Mechanic
The player must bounce them off stars to slow their descent.

Stars act like bumpers or stepping points.

Possible mechanics:
- Tap stars to activate them
- Bounce Maurice and Sprockett upward or sideways
- Chain bounces between stars
- Avoid falling too fast
- Some stars vanish after use
- Some stars move
- Some stars are “star fish” from the Sea of Stars
- Comet trails may push them sideways
- Night Mares may appear above or in the background

### Failure
If Maurice and Sprockett fall off the bottom of the screen:

- life lost
- they reappear higher up
- Maurice is furious
- Sprockett claims it was “mostly controlled falling”

### Tone
This should feel frantic, funny, dreamlike, and slightly scary, but still family-friendly.

## Possible Aardman Permission Easter Egg

If official permission were ever obtained from Aardman, there could be a tiny falling-sequence easter egg.

During the Night Mares falling puzzle, Maurice and Sprockett are plummeting through space after jumping off the Moon of Stilton.

In the distance, a suspiciously familiar homemade moon rocket passes by.

For a brief moment, Maurice and Sprockett screech to a halt mid-fall, look at each other, wiggle their paws in a very Wallace-like panic gesture, then immediately resume plummeting.

This would only be used if proper permission/licensing were granted.

Without permission, the gag should remain a broad original homage: a strange homemade rocket passing in the distance, no copied music, no copied characters, no direct likeness.

The vessel appears to be a pirate ship.

Faint singing drifts across the water:

"From the deep sea of clouds,
to the islands of the moon..."

Maurice looks away.

Pauses.

BLONK.

Looks again.

The ship has vanished.

Later:

Sprockett:
"What?"

Maurice:
"Ship."

Sprockett:
"Where?"

Maurice:

The joke should be quick, dry, and left completely unexplained.
